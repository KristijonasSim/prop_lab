## 2. The goal: find a non-VWAP hypothesis that could beat H-009 (8.9)

Kris asked for a hypothesis that is not VWAP and not a variation on one.

### Killed from the literature before spending time on them

| candidate | why it was dropped |
|---|---|
| **Liquidation cascades** | the best mechanism in crypto — forced sellers are price-insensitive — but Binance has **removed** `liquidationSnapshot` from `data.binance.vision`. Verified by listing the bucket: the prefix returns an empty result while `metrics`, `bookDepth`, `bookTicker`, `aggTrades` all return keys. No long history at any price |
| **Order-book / queue imbalance** | the literature is consistent that the edge is <10bps at 10-second horizons against a 28bps round trip. Cost-limited by construction |
| **Quarter-hour effect** (arXiv 2607.09426) | the paper's own conclusion: ~0.5bps, "one twentieth of a round trip", explicitly non-tradeable as a standalone strategy |
| **Overnight drift** (NY Fed SR-917) | ~100% of the US equity premium in the 02:00–03:00 ET hour — but the same authors published *"The Disappearing Overnight Drift"* in July 2026: ~zero since 2021 |

### H-013 — the derivative against the cash market it tracks

**Mechanism, stated before results.** A perpetual has no expiry, so nothing pulls
it back to spot except basis arbitrageurs and the funding transfer that bills the
crowded side. So: *a move paid for with leverage rather than cash has to be
unwound, because the people holding it are being charged to hold it.*

**Two feeds, both new to this repo, both free, both back to 2020-01:**
`premiumIndexKlines` — the instantaneous `(mark − index)/index`, of which the
8-hourly funding rate H-004 tested is a clamped TWAP — and **spot** klines with
`taker_buy_base`, so cash-side aggression can be compared with the perp's. Every
flow feature here before today was perp-only. `core/basis_data.py` fetches both.

**Stage 1 — the diagnostic (240 cells, same construction as H-006's, imported
rather than reimplemented so the two are read off one ruler):**

| feature | mean abs IC | mean abs spread | beats null | stable across years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **prem_z** | **0.023** | 10.9bps | **15 of 15** | 0.90 |
| lead_4h | 0.016 | 9.7bps | **15 of 15** | 0.88 |
| dprem_4h | 0.014 | 8.1bps | **15 of 15** | 0.88 |
| *H-006's best, `crowd_z`* | *0.022* | *19.6bps* | *53%* | *—* |

Tail edge on BTC, per side, fading the rich tail — monotone in **both**
directions, which is the shape H-008 was killed for lacking:

| tail | 4h | 8h | 24h | 48h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 5.0 | 8.5 | 11.1 | 19.7 |
| 10% | 6.4 | 9.1 | 15.7 | 31.7 |
| 5% | 7.3 | 13.8 | 25.2 | **51.4** |

An earlier pass reported t-statistics of 6–23 on these. **Those were wrong** —
5-minute observations with 24-hour forward windows overlap 288-fold, inflating t
by roughly √288. Sampled one observation per horizon the honest figures are
1.0–2.9, because six years only contains ~122–486 independent 48-hour windows.

**Two confounds ruled out, both cleanly:**

*Is it disguised price reversal?* No. Partial IC controlling `ret_1h` and
`ret_4h` is **unchanged** (24h: −0.0353 raw → −0.0356 partial), and twice the
strength of price reversal alone (−0.0167). The double sort agrees: on BTC the
q1−q5 premium spread is positive inside **all five** recent-return buckets.

*Is it H-009's crowd feed again?* No. Spearman against `crowd_z` is −0.05 (BTC),
−0.12 (ETH), −0.16 (SOL). A different quantity — positioning is who holds the
risk, the premium is what they pay for it.

*Does it generalise past BTC?* Yes, on the diagnostic. Eight further coins:
`prem_z` beats its null in **40 of 40** cells, stable 0.875 — but clears 2x cost
in only 2.5% of them.

**Stage 2 — it fails as a strategy.**

| holds | real clears PF 1.20 @2x | null, per seed | real best | null best |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8h–72h | 3 of 2112 | 2.0 | 1.245 | **1.280** |
| 72h–14d | 196 of 2112 | **203.0** | 2.048 | **2.546** |

The null clears the gate as often as the real signal and its best configuration
scores higher, at every cost level. The direction control barely separates
(contrarian 0.967 vs with-trend 0.951). The long-hold grid was run only because
*every* winner in the first grid sat on the `hold=864` boundary with median PF
rising monotonically across the column — the repo's own rule about a grid cutting
off its answer. Extending it did not rescue it; it just made the null bigger too.

**Stage 3 — it fails as a gate on H-009 as well.** `lead` and `dprem` raise
PF at 2x from 1.806 to ~1.95 and cut drawdown from −51.4R to −33R, but halve
R per day, so return/drawdown *falls* from 37.3 to 30.1. That is the quantity
that sets days-to-funded. H-009's crowd gate moved it the other way (24.6 → 34.4).
`prem_z` as a gate is worse still: what it throws away (1.853) outperforms what
it keeps (1.751).

**Verdict: rejected.** Same shape as H-006 — signal real, strategy dead — except
that unlike H-006 it does not even earn its place as a filter.

## 3. Bugs found and fixed

1. **Millisecond/microsecond timestamps.** The Binance archive switched units
   during 2025 and a batch spanning the switch carries **both**. Deciding the
   unit from the batch maximum reads every millisecond row as microseconds and
   dates it to 1970. Caught because the spot series claimed to start 1970-01-19.
   Fixed per row in `core/basis_data.py`, and the same latent construction in
   `core/binance_metrics.py` was corrected. Existing caches were checked and are
   intact.
2. **`stage1_response.py` overwrote its own baseline.** A wide-universe run wrote
   to the same `stage1_ic.csv` as the core three-coin run it was meant to be
   compared against. Output is now tagged by symbol set.

## 4. H-014 — the Pine rewrite Kris asked for

`pine/pac_pullback_strategy.pine`, Pine v6, rebuilt from JustUncleL's "Scalping
PullBack Tool R1.1". The original is a `study` with **no exit**, so nothing about
it could be scored.

**Defects found in the original:** the fractal functions read raw `high`/`low`
while everything else reads the Heikin Ashi series, so its "use HA in algo
calculations" switch is ignored by half the tool; `alertcondition` fires intrabar
while the arrows can be delayed to bar close, so chart and alert disagree; one
debug plot uses `close` where the logic uses `haClose`; `slowEMA(600)` is computed
and plotted but never used. And the largest one — backtesting it on a Heikin Ashi
chart fills orders at synthetic prices, which is not a price anyone can trade.

**What was added:** strategy form with ATR/structure stops and R targets; a
`runtime.error` guard against non-standard charts; **Inverted and rate-matched
Random control modes**, so the null benchmark can be run inside TradingView; a
report table giving PF at **1x/2x/3x** cost, trades/day, max drawdown in R and
estimated days to +8%, because the Strategy Tester only ever applies one
commission; a minimum stop-distance floor, because a stop at a level price
already grazed divides by ~0 and manufactures 25R winners — a bug this repo has
hit; and eight filters, all default OFF so each can be scored as a paired lift on
the median rather than on whether it produced a new best.

Deliberately absent: any move-to-breakeven option. On this project's data that
cost −0.078 median PF at 0.5R across 5,760 paired configurations.

**It is untested.** It is a price pattern, and price patterns here are 0 for 5.
"Breakout + retest" is already on the known-dead list and a pullback-continuation
entry is structurally the same family. Run the two control modes before anything
else.
