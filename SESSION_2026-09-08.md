## What was built

**Item 1 — kernel fingerprint and automatic staleness.** Every board record now
carries a hash of the kernels, the data, the git SHA and the cost assumption it
was produced from, and `core/build_scoreboard.py` recomputes all of it at render
time. Edit one byte of `strategies/vwap/engine.py` and H-002 renders
`3.0/10 STALE`, naming the file, with nobody editing a note. Two tiers: trade
arithmetic is a hard stale, `core/board.py` and `core/riskladder.py` are a note —
a flag that is always on gets ignored.

**The hole item 1 left, closed the same day.** The fingerprint hashes what a
manifest *declares*, so an undeclared kernel never trips the flag.
`core/manifest_audit.py` walks the import graph statically and fails on anything
reachable but undeclared.

**Item 2 — the verification gate.** `core/verification.py` runs the suite per
hypothesis and writes `verification.json`, stamped with the kernel hash the
evidence was earned on; `core/scorecard.py` caps an unverified record at 3.0.
A **skip is not a pass** — the gate reads junit XML, because "all passed" and
"all skipped" both exit 0.

**Item 3, absorbed.** `pytest` + `hypothesis`, `tests/` with a shared kernel
adapter, golden trade lists, and the repo's first CI.

**Item 4 — one `Strategy` interface.** `core/strategy.py`; both kernels declare

### Bug 5 is the one that moved a number

`RESEARCH_LOG.md` has it in full. Short version: the 2026-09-07 work guarded the
decision bar and the fill bar and stopped. **1,079 of H-002's 17,432 exits and
1,532 of H-016's 8,610 landed on a bar where nothing traded.**

**Which field of the bar the exit rule reads decides how much it cost.** A
trailing stop tests every bar's `high` and `low`, and a padded weekend bar has
`hi == lo ==` the frozen price — so it sits on the trail and force-closes a live
trade. A session-horizon time exit reads only the `close`, and a padded bar's
close *is* the last real close. Same bug; H-016 moved a long way and H-002 did
not move at all.

**And the first estimate had the sign backwards.** Subtracting the R of the
affected trades said dead-bar exits carried +59.03R of H-016's +127.08R — i.e.
that fixing it would halve the edge. Re-running did the opposite: they were
premature stop-outs at a price nobody traded. **The counterfactual to a
fill-assumption bug is a re-run, not a subtraction.**

### Bug 8 is the one that took a headline away

Fixing the dead-bar exits let positions ride through the closed weekend — which
immediately raised the question the old behaviour had hidden: **at what price
does it resolve?** Both kernels filled a triggered stop AT the stop level on any
bar whose range contained it. On a bar that OPENED beyond the stop that is wrong;
a stop is a stop-market order and the fill is the open.

| | before either fix | after the exit fix | after the gap fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-016 expected days | 175.9 | 127.6 | **260.1** |
| H-016 cells clearing PF 1.20 at 2x | 12/16 | 14/16 | 13/16 |
| H-016 vs its null | 12 vs 5.0 | 14 vs 3.3 | 13 vs 3.0 |
| H-002 expected days | 143.6 | 143.6 | 143.6 |

**H-002 is immune, H-016 is not, and the reason is structural.** H-002 is
session-bound and rarely holds across a weekend — 1 gapped stop in 5,382. H-016's
entire exit is a multi-day trailing stop — 168 gapped of 2,027, median 13.9–27.2bps
through, worst 216bps.

**H-016's pace headline was resting on a fill nobody could have got.** The edge
survives (13 of 16 cells against a null's 3.0). The speed never existed.

A **target** deliberately keeps its level: it is a limit order, a gap through it
fills better, and taking that bonus would be optimism in the other direction.
