## 1. THE NOISE FLOOR IS ON THE BOARD — `core/noiseband.py`

The 2026-09-08 session measured a noise floor (six information-free gates, 13.3
to 26.5 expected days) and then left the board quoting point estimates against
it. That was the top of its own to-do list and it is now built.

**What a band is.** Resample one cell's daily returns with a stationary block
bootstrap (mean block 10 trading days, so a bad fortnight survives as a bad
fortnight), re-run the SAME prop simulation on each resample, and report the
10th–90th percentile of the answer. 200 resamples, about 70ms per band.

**Why it is trustworthy.** `_states` is `riskladder.run_accounts` with the
account loop vectorised, and `tests/test_noiseband.py` pins the two against each
other account for account — pass rate, both breach rates, still-open share and
median days, at three position sizes. A band computed under different rules from
the point estimate it brackets would be worse than no band.

**What it changes on the page.**

* Every ranked number carries its band in small type underneath — days and pass
  rate both, because Kris's goal is the pair.
* A band is stored **per ladder rung**, so forcing a different risk moves the
  band with the number. `core/repick.py` carries `band` through a policy change
  for the same reason: a headline from one rung under another rung's band is a
  worse lie than no band at all.
* Rows whose bands overlap share a **TIER** and `core/scorecard.rank_tiers`
  refuses to order them. Tiers form against the tier LEADER, not the neighbour:
  band overlap is not transitive, and comparing neighbours chains a whole table
  into one tier through a run of wide bands.
* The measured floor is drawn at the top of the page, and a headline inside it
  is flagged on the row.

**What it is not.** It is not the selection band — choosing the best of ten
markets adds uncertainty a per-cell bootstrap cannot see. The paired null stays
the primary gate.

## 4. BOTH SECOND-ENGINE DISAGREEMENTS ARE RESOLVED — NEITHER WAS A LOOK-AHEAD

They had been carried as open for a day, reported as counts (4 trades of 330, 1
of 416) rather than as mechanisms. Printing both engines' state at the disputed
bar answered both in minutes.

### 30m, MODE_PULLBACK and MODE_RECLAIM, anchor 13:30 — the RULE was ill-posed

Both modes ask whether the PREVIOUS bar closed above or below its VWAP. All four
disputed decisions sit on the first live bar after the weekend, so the previous
bar is Dukascopy padding: O=H=L=C at one frozen price for the whole session,
which makes the session VWAP **exactly that price**. The true answer is neither
above nor below.

What decided it instead was the last bit of the accumulation — pandas
`groupby().cumsum()` in the kernel against an incremental sum in the port:

| bar | close − VWAP | one ulp |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-04-07 21:30 | 0.000e+00 | 4.5e-13 |
| 2025-04-06 21:30 | 0.000e+00 | 4.5e-13 |
| 2025-05-18 21:30 | −4.547e-13 | 4.5e-13 |
| 2025-06-15 21:30 | +4.547e-13 | 4.5e-13 |

`engine.PX_EPS_FRAC = 1e-9` gives the comparison a tolerance in price terms: a
strict test must beat the floor, a non-strict one absorbs it. 1e-9 of price is
3e-6 on gold — seven orders above the noise, two below one tick.

Blast radius, measured before the change: 3,095 of 45,024 30m bars are ties at
that anchor and **all but 2 are padded bars**, so this reaches the weekend open
and nothing else. Rolling anchors have zero ties at any timeframe.

### 15m, MODE_BREAK, rolling 384-bar anchor — the PORT's bookkeeping

Both engines signalled the short and both entered at bar 90041. The kernel caps a
hold at the end of the session (`horizon = stop_bar`) and books the trade at the
last usable bar; the port left the horizon uncapped and then **dropped whatever
was still open when the stream stopped**. One trade of 416 — the last one,
entered seven bars from the end. The port now caps at `n_bars - 1`.

### The nightly slow suite is green end to end for the first time

`pytest -m slow`: **7 passed**. 1h, 30m, 15m, 5m and the ribbon checks.

**The general lesson:** an entry-bar disagreement is not automatically a
causality bug. One of these was a rule with no answer; the other was a convention
at the end of the data. Both were invisible while the disagreement was reported
as a count.

## 6. WHAT THE BANDS SAY ABOUT THE BOARD, now that they exist

**Fourteen of H-027's twenty cells are in one tier.** From SOLUSDT 1h at 13.9
expected days to BTCUSDT 1h at 23.8, no pair can be told apart at 10–90%.

That tier contains XAUUSD 1h at **PF@2x 1.715** and GBPUSD 4h at **0.692** - a
cell that makes money at double cost and one that loses at it, indistinguishable
on the metric the board ranks by. The reason is in the definition:
`expected_days = median_days / pass_rate`, and at a 2% position a losing book
still funds the occasional account on variance before it dies. Speed does not
know the difference. **Profit factor at 2x and the paired null still separate
these cells; expected days does not.**

That is a criticism of the ranking, not of the strategy. It is also exactly what
the noise floor predicted, now visible per cell instead of as one measured
number in a markdown file.
