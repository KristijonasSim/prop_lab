## 4. The three that were built

### H-024 — book depth imbalance. FAILS ON COST.

`core/binance_depth.py`, `strategies/depth/stage1_response.py`.

The signal is **real**. On BTCUSDT, 2023-01 to 2026-09, 85 feature x horizon
cells:

| cell | spread | monotone | years same sign | beats null |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `imbxthin_1` @4h | 4.1 bps | 0.75 | 4/4 | yes |
| `imbz_5` @4h | 3.9 bps | 0.50 | 3/4 | yes |
| `imbz_2` @4h | 2.5 bps | **1.00** | 4/4 | yes |

Direction is **with** the resting book — more bid than ask, higher forward
return. The thin-book interaction behaves exactly as the mechanism predicts:
imbalance pays more when there is less book behind it.

**And 0 of 85 cells clear even the cheapest gate (8bps maker, doubled).** Best
spread in the entire grid is 4.6bps. This is the same 1-7bps band as H-021
(2.67bps) and H-022 (6.55bps).

Two data caveats found while building this, both worth knowing:

- **The +/-0.2% band only exists from 2026-01-15.** For 82.6% of the history the
  finest level is 1%. So `imbz_0.2` and `imbxthin_0.2` are measured on eight
  months, not three and a half years — their "1/1 years" column says so. The
  headline cells (`imbz_2`, `imbz_5`, `imbxthin_1`) use bands present throughout
  and are unaffected.
- A missing band must not advance the inner edge when walking the book, or every
  fill floors at 20bps. That bug was in the first cost run and is fixed.

### H-024 stage 2 — the cost model. THE USEFUL HALF, and it worked.

`strategies/depth/stage2_cost.py`. One-way market impact, median bps, measured
by walking the real resting book:

| clip | BTCUSDT | ETHUSDT | SOLUSDT | XRPUSDT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10k | 0.004 | 0.009 | 0.035 | 0.075 |
| **$50k** | **0.018** | **0.044** | **0.174** | **0.374** |
| $250k | 0.092 | 0.218 | 0.870 | 1.868 |
| $1M | 0.368 | 0.872 | 3.481 | 7.471 |

**The repo assumes 2bps of slippage per side. The measured impact at a prop-sized
clip on BTC is 0.018bps — roughly a hundredth of the assumption.**

Three consequences:

1. The 14bps round trip is **fee plus spread, essentially not impact**. Impact
   only becomes material above ~$1M, and on the thin coins.
2. That makes the cap-curve argument cheaper than it looked. XRP costs 20x more
   impact than BTC and 20x almost nothing is still almost nothing.
3. The true round trip is probably **below 14bps**, which moves every marginal
   result in this repo slightly in our favour. It does not rescue anything that
   failed by a factor of two, and H-023 already showed that even a zero fee does
   not reach the phase target.

Not measured here: the bid-ask spread itself. `bookTicker` would give it and the
archive stopped publishing it in 2024-03. At the p99 bar the picture changes for
thin coins — SOL at $1M pays 53.8bps round trip — so size limits are real even
though the median is negligible.

### TASK 3 — do the weak signals stack? NO, but it found a lead.

`strategies/stack/stage1_combine.py`. Weights and signs fitted on the first half
only, scored on the second, week-block null, benchmark = the best single
ingredient measured on the same test half.

**The feeds are nearly independent** — median pairwise |corr| **0.027** across 16
features, **0.036** across 22. That was the precondition, and it holds.

**And stacking still does not work.** Only **6 of 30** combinations beat their own
best ingredient; the **median lift is −3.10 bps**. Equal weighting dilutes two
real signals with twenty noisy ones — the same mechanism H-012 found when a wider
book got slower. Train IC does not carry to test, so IC-weighting and top-k
selection both underperform too. 0 of 30 cells clear 14bps.

**The lead:** the benchmark the combinations had to beat was
**`size_z` at 4h = 14.6 bps on the held-out half** — `sum_toptrader_long_short_ratio`,
the largest accounts weighted by position size. First cell measured in this

on the test half, so the number is biased upward; `strategies/stack/stage2_size.py`
re-measures it across 11 coins against 1d/1w/1mo block nulls.

### TASK 4 — the target spec. The evaluation itself had never been nulled.

`core/target_spec.py`. This repo scores every hypothesis against a null and has
caught three results that way. Nobody had ever done it to the prop challenge.

**At literally zero edge**, under one-step rules (8% target, 4% daily, 8% max):

| max-loss rule | 1 account @0.5% risk | **at least one of three** |
|---|---|---|
| trailing (what the repo models) | 40.2% | **78.6%** |
| static only (what many firms use) | 57.1% | **92.1%** |

**A coin flip funds an account.** So pass rate on its own is nearly uninformative
— what an edge has to buy is the LIFT over that line, and the thing that actually
matters is keeping the account afterwards, which no amount of luck does.

Two more results worth having:

- **Optimal risk is 0.5-1% per trade**, and it is roughly invariant to edge size.
- **Above that the DAILY loss limit is what kills accounts, not the max loss** —
  at 3% risk, 71% of zero-edge accounts die on the daily rule.

**Code finding.** `PropRules.trailing` and `.static` are documented as
configurable but `core/riskladder.run_accounts` ORs both breach conditions and
reads neither flag. Since `peak >= 0` the trailing term is always the stricter,
so the OR is decided by trailing alone: `static` is redundant and
`trailing=False` is silently ignored. Every board number used the trailing
reading — which is what the docstring intends, so no number is wrong — but the
knob does not work, and the rule is worth 17 points of zero-edge pass rate.

### TASK 2 — the real venue cost. A false claim, corrected.

`core/fx_data.py` opened with: bars are built from the MID price, the mean
half-spread is kept alongside, and therefore "the cost model for FX and gold is
measured from the data rather than assumed".

**None of it was true.** It fetches `BID_candles_min_1.bi5` — bid-only candles —
and the cached parquets carry exactly `open, high, low, close, volume`. Gold's
cost is an assumption like every other, and a long entry read off a bid bar is
optimistic by the full spread. Docstring corrected.

`core/fx_spread.py` now measures it from Dukascopy's hourly tick files. Early
read, 33 sampled hours: **XAUUSD spread 1.68 bps of mid = 0.84 bps per side,
against 1.50 assumed**, and flat across the UTC day (1.54-1.83). Commission on
top: cTrader raw is about $6 round turn per lot, which on a 100oz gold lot at
$4,000 is 0.15bps round trip.

**So gold's cost assumption is roughly 1.8x conservative and its results are
understated, not overstated.** The full 120-day, three-instrument run is finishing.

### A correction to the three-account plan

The target-spec work assumed three accounts are independent draws. **They are not
if they run the same strategy** — three accounts trading one gold book are
perfectly correlated and all pass or fail together, so the 1-(1-p)^3 arithmetic
does not apply. Kris's plan of 3-7 *different* strategies on separate evaluations
is the version that works; three copies of one book buys nothing but three fees.
